Method notes

By Ulrich Djidonou. The question, approach, and limitations behind three public projects.

Measuring economic uncertainty

ICIE

Question
What can online searches tell us about economic uncertainty in Canada?
Approach
I present an index based on Google Trends, its sensitivity tests and external validation in a public dashboard.
Interpretation limits
The index measures a signal from online searches. It does not directly measure all the uncertainty experienced by households and firms, nor does it establish a causal relationship.
Explore the results and methodology (French)

Automating monitoring, retaining judgment

La Frontière

Question
How can we identify research connecting economics, artificial intelligence, and machine learning?
Approach
The monitoring workflow combines Python collection, thematic selection, AI-generated summaries, and automated checks. Links to original texts let readers return to the source.
Interpretation limits
Selection and summaries are automated; they may miss a paper or simplify its argument. They help identify relevant work; reading the source is necessary to assess its methods and findings.
Read the feed and its methodology

Connecting news and data

GeoEcon Pulse

Question
How can we follow geoeconomic developments across several regions in one tool?
Approach
The dashboard brings together indicators and news for Canada, the United States, China, India, and the world. Readers can move from the overview to the sources.
Interpretation limits
An updated dashboard does not mean all indicators cover the same period. Placing news alongside indicators does not establish a causal link.
