### Methodology

## A Four-Step Work Process

1. **Formulate the question.** Clarify the economic, institutional, or strategic problem.
2. **Build the evidence.** Select the appropriate data, methodology, and validation techniques.
3. **Make the analysis actionable.** Produce a dashboard, model, report, pipeline, or interactive tool.
4. **Communicate.** Clearly explain what the results show, what they do not show, and what decisions they enable.

This approach has been applied in public projects, from the ICIE index to the La Frontière monitoring initiative.

# La Frontière

*What is La Frontière?*

La Frontière is an automated monitoring service that tracks articles at the intersection of artificial intelligence, economics, and machine learning.

Button: Follow La Frontière (RSS)

Links: RSS Feed | Feed Data (JSON) | Resources

### How this monitoring service is produced (click to read)

First, a Python pipeline collects new publications twice a week (Mondays and Thursdays) from arXiv (econ.EM, econ.GN, cs.LG, cs.CL, stat.ML—filtered for economics), the NBER, the journals of the American Economic Association (AER, JEP, JEL, AEJ), VoxEU/CEPR, the Bank of Canada, the U.S. Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank, and the International Monetary Fund. It then assigns a thematic score to each entry: the main selection includes those with a score of at least 3.

Next, for recognized economic journals and institutions (AER, JEP, JEL, AEJ, NBER, VoxEU/CEPR, Bank of Canada, U.S. Federal Reserve, European Central Bank, International Monetary Fund), the economic nature of the text is assumed: only the connection to artificial intelligence remains to be evaluated, so as not to exclude an article simply because its abstract lacks standard economic vocabulary.

Finally, French abstracts are generated by a language model (Gemini, supplemented by Claude), then checked by automated validators: any missing figure in the title or source abstract results in the text being rejected.

The “Featured Article of the Week” is the article with the highest thematic score among those reported in the most recent data collection, not among the ninety-day selection period: without this freshness requirement, a highly rated article would remain in that spot for weeks. A minimum threshold is also applied. When no recent article achieves a sufficient score, the section indicates this rather than highlighting a text that does not deserve it.

Two terms recur in the entry labels. A **research paper** is a work released prior to publication, such as a preprint or working paper. A **published article** has appeared in a peer-reviewed journal or in an editorial publication.

In addition, I review the pipeline’s output whenever possible. If you notice an anomaly or see room for improvement in any area, please write to me: your feedback is welcome.

### Page Sections

- Highlight of the Week (title and caption generated automatically)
- Thematic Breakdown, Last 90 Days
- Search field: “ Search the feed...”; “Return to main selection” button
- Current monitoring, **Main selection**: This automatic selection includes entries with a thematic score of at least 3.
- Archives, **Comprehensive archive**: The archive retains all entries identified by the thematic collection, including those that fall below the threshold for the main selection.

Note: Feed entries, the “Signal of the Week,” and the archives are generated by the pipeline from `frontiere/data/flux.json`. This content changes with each collection and is therefore not fixed here.

### Careers in Economics

### AI Tools for Economic Research

- **Elicit**: An AI platform that allows users to search, summarize, extract data, and interact with over 125 million scientific articles.
- **Consensus**: An AI-based academic search engine that synthesizes findings from the scientific literature to answer a research question.
- **Connected Papers**: A visualization tool that maps the links between academic articles to explore a research field starting from a single paper.

### MCP Servers and Data Access

- **FRED API (Federal Reserve Economic Data)**: An API from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis providing access to hundreds of thousands of U.S. and international economic and financial time series.
- **Statistics Canada, Web Data Service (WDS)**: An official API providing programmatic access to data and metadata published every business day by Statistics Canada.
- **O*NET OnLine**: A U.S. government database de

- **O*NET OnLine**: A U.S. government database describing more than 900 occupations, often used to measure the susceptibility of jobs to automation and AI.
- **Anthropic Economic Index**: A research program by Anthropic that documents how generative AI is used in the economy based on anonymized Claude conversations.

### Causal and ML Libraries

- **EconML**: A Python library from Microsoft that estimates heterogeneous treatment effects from observational data by combining econometrics and machine learning.
- **DoWhy**: A Python library for causal inference that makes causal assumptions explicit and allows them to be tested before estimating an effect.
- **PyFixest**: A Python library for high-dimensional fixed-effects regression, modeled after the syntax of the R package `fixest`, and actively maintained.
- **XGBoost**: A distributed gradient boosting library, widely used for forecasting and classification on large-scale economic data.
- **Prophet**: An additive model-based time series forecasting library maintained by Meta, accounting for seasonality and holiday effects.

### Foundational Papers on AI and Economics

- **GPTs are GPTs (Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin, Rock, 2023)**: An OpenAI study estimating that approximately 80 percent of the U.S. workforce could see at least 10 percent of their tasks affected by large language models.
- **Anthropic Economic Index report: Uneven geographic and enterprise AI adoption**: A report documenting the uses of Claude in over 150 countries and across enterprises, based on two million anonymized conversations.
- **Which Economic Tasks Are Performed with AI? Evidence from Millions of Claude Conversations**: A large-scale analysis of economic tasks actually performed through conversations with Claude, classified according to the O*NET taxonomy.

### Courses and Training

- **Machine Learning & Causal Inference (Stanford GSB)**: A short course from the Stanford Graduate School of Business combining machine learning and causal inference for the analysis of randomized trials.
- **Machine Learning & Data Science for Economists (NABE)**: A hands-on course from the National Association for Business Economics designed for practicing economists, updated in 2026 with Python and code agents.

# Frequently Asked Questions

**What exactly does an economist do?**
An economist translates a question into a measurable problem, then typically answers it using data and statistical methods (e.g., econometrics, causal inference, forecasting, causal AI, machine learning). The result takes the form of a note, a research report, an assessment, an indicator, a model, or a dashboard that can be used to inform decision-making.

**What tools do you use for your analyses?**
Mainly Python, R, Stata, and SQL for analysis and econometrics; machine learning models such as XGBoost; forecasting methods such as ARIMA and Prophet; Power BI or interactive web pages for dashboards; and AI tools such as Claude Code, Google Antigravity, Codex, Ollama, and Perplexity.

**Do you work in French and English?**
Yes, I work in both languages. My public projects on GitHub are generally documented in French and/or English, depending on the project.

**A must-read book for an economist?**
Bellemare, Marc F. (2022), *Doing Economics: What You Should Have Learned in Grad School–But Didn’t*, Cambridge, MA: MIT Press. I especially recommend the chapter “Writing Papers” (an earlier version of the chapter can be found here).
